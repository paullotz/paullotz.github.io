## Summary & Synopsis
A guide to self-directed learning focused on direct practice, active recall, targeted drills, and spaced repetition.

## Key Takeaways & Detailed Notes

### 1. Active Recall over Passive Review
- Closed-book recall, self-testing, and flashcards build deeper retention than passive rereading.
- Learning is more effective when you put yourself through the slight friction of having to retrieve concepts from memory.

### 2. Drills for Bottlenecks
- Break down a skill into distinct components and practice specific bottlenecks (like pointer arithmetic, recursion, or algorithm traces) instead of just repeating entire workflows.

### 3. Procrastination & Memory Retention
- When facing procrastination, commit to starting for just five minutes, take a breath, and ease into a timed focus block.
- Spaced review prevents rapid drop-off in memory retention.
- Keep experimenting with different resources, formats, and study techniques. Start by emulating working patterns, then build your own.
