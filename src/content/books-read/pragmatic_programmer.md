## Summary & Synopsis
A classic guide on engineering discipline, pragmatic system design, defensive programming, and long-term code maintenance.

## Key Takeaways & Detailed Notes

### 1. Core Principles
- **Easier to Change (ETC):** Good software is structured so that future modifications require minimal resistance.
- **Fix Broken Windows:** Fix messy code, broken builds, and neglected modules immediately before poor quality becomes the team standard.
- **DRY (Don't Repeat Yourself):** Every distinct piece of knowledge in a codebase should have a single, unambiguous representation.
- **Orthogonality & Loose Coupling:** Changes to one component should not trigger unexpected side effects in unrelated modules.

### 2. Practical Design Patterns & Tools
- **State Machines & Strategy Patterns:** Modeling state explicitly helps replace messy nested conditionals.
- **Message Passing & Decoupled Workers:** Asynchronous tasks stay reliable when coordinated through clear message passing.
- **Know Your Audience:** Code comments, docs, and interface designs should serve the people who maintain and read them.
