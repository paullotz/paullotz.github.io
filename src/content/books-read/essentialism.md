# Book Notes: Essentialism

### Core Philosophy
* **Essentialism:** Doing less but better ("weniger aber besser").
* **Focus Energy:** Concentrate effort on the few areas that deliver real benefit instead of scattering attention across ten different directions.
* **Vital Few vs. Trivial Many:** Focus on the few things that matter most.
* **Ownership:** If you don't prioritize your time, other people will do it for you.

### Decision Making & Saying No
* **The 90% Rule:** Pick carefully. Ask "Do I really want this?" Proceed only when you are 90% sure.
* **Clear Logic:** If it isn't a clear yes, treat it as a clear no.
* **Trade-offs:** Favor a fast no and a deliberate yes. Saying yes to one request means turning down others.
* **Social Impact:** Turning down requests might feel uncomfortable in the moment, but clear boundaries earn respect over time.
* **Eliminate Non-Essentials:** Cut unnecessary meetings and busywork to protect time for substantial work.

### Mindset & Productivity
* **Big Picture:** Step back from small details and look at overall direction.
* **Play & Creativity:** Unstructured play helps the mind recharge. What gave you energy when you were younger?
* **Choice:** Shift from "I have to" to "I choose to."
* **Mental Space:** Reserve dedicated blocks each day for focused thinking and give yourself permission to be bored.
* **Sunk Cost Bias:** Watch out for emotional attachment to past efforts or projects that no longer make sense.
* **Zero-Based Budgeting:** Build your schedule from scratch rather than filling gaps around existing obligations.
* **The Endowment Effect:** Ask yourself: "If I didn't already have this, how much would I pay to get it?"

### Execution & Progress
* **Remove Constraints:** Find the main bottleneck in your workflow and address it directly.
* **Small Wins:** Track steady progress and recognize incremental improvements. Tangible momentum keeps motivation high.
* **Habits:** Identify the cues that start distracting or unproductive routines.
* **Boundaries:** Note deal-breakers whenever you notice boundaries being crossed.
* **Clarity:** Keep asking: "How will we know when this is done?"
